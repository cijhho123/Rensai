The moment Suruga Kanbaru starts narrating in her own voice, something quietly breaks open. Not because of anything supernatural on screen. Because you suddenly realize how completely you've misread her.

For three arcs across the main Monogatari series — Suruga Monkey, the Suruga Devil follow-up in Hanamonogatari, the glimpses in Zoku Owarimonogatari — you watched her through Koyomi Araragi's perspective.^[[Nisio Isin, *Bakemonogatari* vol. 2 (Kodansha Box, 2006; English: Vertical Inc., 2016). The Suruga Monkey arc establishes Kanbaru as a character mediated entirely through Araragi's point of view.]] You saw the star athlete who tackles him in hallways as a greeting, the underclassman carrying a monstrous wrapped arm beneath a basketball jersey, the girl whose love for Hitagi Senjogahara is older than anything Araragi can claim and complicated in ways he processes primarily as embarrassment and fascination. He intervened. The Rainy Devil's arm was handled. Done. Kanbaru was saved.

*Suruga Bonehead* — the Kanbaru-narrated arc from *Orokamonogatari*, adapted in the first cour of *Monogatari Series: Off & Monster Season*^[[Crystalyn Hodgkins, "Monogatari Series' Off Season, Monster Season Novels Get Anime," *Anime News Network*, January 18, 2024, https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2024-01-18/monogatari-series-off-season-monster-season-novels-get-anime/.206608]] — makes one argument with extraordinary precision: all of that was triage. The apparition was removed. Kanbaru was not healed. The real work happened after Araragi graduated and stopped being there to do anything at all.

## What Araragi's Gaze Was Doing

The Monogatari franchise has always been scrupulous about the costs of its narrative architecture. First-person narration as the series' structural spine means every character is filtered — distorted, idealized, occasionally hallucinated — through whoever is telling the story at that moment. Shaft leans into this formally: the show fills its frames with on-screen text, with glimpses of the narrator's inner monologue as typographic event, making the subjectivity of perspective into a visual argument rather than just a structural one.^[[Nick Creamer, "Monogatari is a Disaster," *Anime News Network*, August 7, 2015, https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2015-08-07/monogatari-is-a-disaster/.90575]] Araragi narrating Kanbaru means you get: her speed, her physicality, her casual sexual provocation (which he simultaneously enjoys and pretends to be scandalized by), her monstrousness. What you do not get is her interior. When the Rainy Devil arc resolves, the camera — Araragi's attention — moves on.

The Rainy Devil itself is textbook Monogatari apparition logic: a supernatural manifestation of an emotional state that cannot be consciously acknowledged. Kanbaru doesn't consciously want to hurt Araragi. But she holds, at some depth she cannot fully access, a wish that everything blocking Hitagi's happiness would be destroyed — and a willingness to be destroyed along with it.^[[Nisio Isin, *Bakemonogatari* vol. 2. The monkey's paw mechanism in Suruga Monkey is explicit: it does not distinguish between desires the host acknowledges and those they suppress.]] The monkey's paw doesn't make moral distinctions. It acts on the whole wish, not just the part she'd admit to. Her arm becomes an agent of her own subconscious death drive, going after Araragi because at some level she's invited it to.

The franchise's own diagnostic system is explicit about this. Meme Oshino explains it in Bakemonogatari, and every subsequent arc confirms it: apparitions are not invasions. They are crystallizations — emotions that have become too heavy to remain interior, finding a supernatural form that allows them to act in the world.^[[Nisio Isin, *Bakemonogatari* vol. 1 (Kodansha Box, 2006; English: Vertical Inc., 2016). Oshino's foundational explanation of apparitions as the host's own creation, not external phenomena.]] Araragi removes the crystal. He is very good at removing the crystal. What he has never been equipped to address is why the crystal formed in the first place, because that work belongs to the person who formed it.

## What the Arc Left Intact

Araragi fixes Kanbaru's arm. He takes the Rainy Devil into his own body, absorbs its damage, and with Meme Oshino's involvement the apparition is ultimately dispelled.^[[Nisio Isin, *Bakemonogatari* vol. 2.]] The physical resolution is clean. What the arc cannot touch:

The unrequited love for Hitagi, which predates Araragi's existence in both their lives. The psychology of someone who constructs devotion as self-erasure — love expressed not as desire for reciprocation but as a willingness to destroy yourself in service to the beloved. The identity built around being exceptional enough (at basketball, at academics, at wit) that she never has to ask for anything, only give. The tendency, baked deep, to make her vulnerability legible only in extreme and indirect forms — not "I am hurting" but "my arm is a monster."

None of this is resolved by the Rainy Devil's departure. Araragi himself cannot see most of it, because he experiences Kanbaru primarily as someone to be protected and occasionally as someone slightly terrifying. His narration gives her intelligence, athleticism, loyalty, and a particular kind of frank eroticism. It does not give her a private psychology that exists when he isn't watching. That is what *Suruga Bonehead* is for.

## The Year After the Rescuer Leaves

*Off & Monster Season*'s first cour is a set of stories all sharing one structural premise: what happens to the people Araragi helped when he is no longer there?^[[Egan Loo, "Monogatari Series Off & Monster Season Anime's Promo Video Reveals July 6 Debut," *Anime News Network*, May 3, 2024, https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2024-05-03/monogatari-series-off-and-monster-season-anime-promo-video-reveals-july-6-debut/.210470]] He has graduated. He is in university, a fact that matters to the stories primarily by his absence from them. *Suruga Bonehead* takes place during Kanbaru's final year of high school — she is now the upperclassman, the one with reputation, the person younger students come to with problems they can't take to teachers.

This is not an accident. Nisio Isin has put Kanbaru in the structural role Araragi used to occupy.^[[Nisio Isin, *Orokamonogatari* (Kodansha Box, 2015).]] She is the one other characters bring strange situations to. She solves things, deflects things, mediates between people who can't talk to each other directly. She does it in her own idiom — faster, funnier, more physically present than Araragi ever was — but the architecture is recognizable. What the arc is quietly asking is: was this always there, just waiting for Araragi to vacate the space?

The answer is yes. And the answer is uncomfortable in the best way, because it retroactively rewrites the entire Suruga Monkey arc. Kanbaru was never primarily a person who needed saving. She was a person with a symptom that was acutely dangerous and required immediate intervention — and Araragi provided that intervention — but the person had already existed before the symptom and continued existing after it. The arc had always been as much about Araragi's need to save as it was about Kanbaru's need to be saved.

## First-Person as Diagnostic Tool

The specific structural move *Orokamonogatari* makes with *Suruga Bonehead* — giving Kanbaru first-person narration — is where the arc's argument becomes most precise. What she sounds like when narrating herself is categorically different from what she sounds like when Araragi narrates her.

Araragi's Kanbaru is a force of nature, slightly chaotic, using self-deprecation strategically. Kanbaru's Kanbaru is *precise*. She knows exactly what she's doing and why. She tracks the emotional dynamics around her with a clarity that Araragi's narration actively obscured, because Araragi processes her through his protective instinct and his attraction and his guilt about the arm. She doesn't process herself through any of that. She simply sees clearly.^[[Nisio Isin, *Orokamonogatari*.]]

The unrequited love for Hitagi is still there. *Suruga Bonehead* doesn't resolve it by removing it — it resolves it by showing that Kanbaru has learned to hold it without it becoming a death wish. The feeling is real. It remains real. What has changed is that she is no longer asking her subconscious to act on it through a supernatural proxy. She has, at some point in the years since Bakemonogatari, decided that the love is hers to carry and not something that should damage anyone else.

That decision isn't narrated in any arc. The anime doesn't show us the moment it happened, because it didn't happen in a moment. It accumulated across the gap between the main series and Off Season — across normal life, basketball practice, studying, minor social dramas with classmates. Across the undramatic work of becoming an adult.^[[Crystalyn Hodgkins, "Monogatari Off & Monster Season Anime Gets New Episode," *Anime News Network*, July 3, 2025, https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-07-03/monogatari-off-and-monster-season-anime-gets-new-episode/.226264]] This is the franchise's most honest acknowledgment of how recovery actually functions. It's not an arc. It's not a climax with a speech. It's the time between the arcs, which no series can dramatize and which Off Season can only gesture at through the fact of Kanbaru already having done it.

## The Intervention Was Leaving

Monogatari is, at its foundations, a series about the costs and limits of its own protagonist's desire to help.^[[Wikipedia contributors, "Monogatari (series)," *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogatari_(series). The series' thematic arc from Bakemonogatari through Owarimonogatari traces Araragi's growth from instinctive intervener to someone who learns the difference between helping and entangling.]] The franchise has traced this in Araragi's relationships with almost every character — his help is necessary, it is also structurally entangling, it makes him the center of situations where he arguably should not be central. Ougi exists, in part, as an externalization of everything that's wrong with this tendency. The main series ends with Araragi in something like balance, but only because the people around him have pushed back against his helpfulness enough to demand their own space.

*Suruga Bonehead* is the proof of concept. It is Monogatari demonstrating, with a character we know well, that the resolution the franchise calls for doesn't happen *in* a dramatic arc — it happens *after*, in the ordinary life that dramatic arcs are never equipped to show. The Rainy Devil was dangerous. Its removal was necessary. It was also exactly one step of a much longer process. And the only thing required for the rest of that process was that Araragi not be there for it.

The *Off & Monster Season* anime licensed by Crunchyroll^[[Kyle Cardine, "Crunchyroll Summer 2024 Anime Season Lineup Announced!," *Crunchyroll*, June 18, 2024, https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/seasonal-lineup/2024/6/18/summer-2024-anime-crunchyroll]] has received considerable attention for its visual experimentation, for YOASOBI's *Undead* ending theme,^[[Crystalyn Hodgkins, "YOASOBI Performs New Monogatari Series Anime's Theme Song," *Anime News Network*, May 2, 2024, https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2024-05-02/yoasobi-performs-new-monogatari-series-anime-theme-song/.210427]] for director Midori Yoshizawa's distinctive material-based visual language, for what it means that the franchise continues past Owarimonogatari.^[[Wikipedia contributors, "Monogatari (series)," *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogatari_(series)]] These are all legitimate discussions. But *Suruga Bonehead* is doing something that none of those discussions quite touches: it is giving us, finally, the second half of a story the franchise started in 2009 and didn't finish on purpose.

The arm was never the problem. Kanbaru always knew that. Now, for the first time, she gets to tell us so herself.
